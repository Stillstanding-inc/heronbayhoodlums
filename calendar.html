The working-water calendar

Out here the year runs on seasons — when the oysters open, when the shrimp run, when the waters rest. These dates and times come from the Alabama Marine Resources Division (MRD), the authority that sets them. Seasons change by the Division's announcements, sometimes in the middle of a run — so treat this page as your planning calendar, and always confirm before you harvest. MRD: (251) 861-2882.

🦪 Oyster season — CLOSED November 18

Alabama's oyster season opened Monday, November 2, 2026, limited to the Cedar Point East, Cedar Point West, and Heron Bay grids, harvest hours Monday–Friday, 7 a.m.–2 p.m., 8 sacks a day commercial. MRD has now called the end: all public water bottoms close to oyster harvest at 2 p.m. on Wednesday, November 18, 2026 — the season ran just 12 days. The 2026 commercial harvest is expected to come in under 400 sacks (against nearly 25,000 in 2024 and 5,000 in 2025), and the closure protects the remaining spawning population while the reefs recover. Do not harvest after 2 p.m. Wednesday.

ON NOW — October 24 through December 31, every year

🐟 Roe mullet season

When the mullet run heavy with roe, Alabama law calls it: from October 24 through December 31, every year — no roe mullet may be taken in Alabama waters outside the season, and you may not possess roe mullet in Alabama waters during the closed season (except those imported under 220-3-.03). No person may take or possess roe mullet in Alabama waters from January 1 through October 23. The rule protects the spawning run so the next year's fish are there.

Through the year — the rhythm of this coast

  • Spring — The bay wakes up: fish move in, the water warms, the shrimp start to grow, and the work starts early.
  • Summer — Shrimp season opens in the inside waters (in 2026: June 1, 6 a.m.), the bay is full of boats, and the days are long.
  • Fall — The oyster season opens in the fall (in 2026: opened November 2, closed November 18). Roe mullet season runs October 24 through December 31.
  • Winter — The water rests, the season is set by MRD, and the fleet mends nets, traps, and boats for the next run.

Annual community dates (the big gatherings — the Blessing of the Fleet, the Seafood Festival, the Mardi Gras parades) move year to year; when the next one is set, it will be posted here.
